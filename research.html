Research

Which observables probe the ultraviolet structure of spacetime, and which phenomena can be explained without specifying it?

This question has guided my research since the beginning of my PhD. Reconciling General Relativity and Quantum Field Theory requires not only mathematical consistency, but also a clear understanding of which physical questions really need a quantum theory of gravity, and which can be answered within classical or semiclassical frameworks.

From UV completeness to low-energy physics

A candidate theory of quantum gravity must connect to low-energy physics and observables. I use the Renormalization Group and symmetry arguments to understand how the UV structure of gravity translates into an effective field theory description.

Charting black holes with the Renormalization Group

Using UV-complete RG trajectories in asymptotically safe gravity, I constrain the Wilson coefficients of the gravitational effective action and hence the space of admissible black hole spacetimes. This led to a phase diagram of static solutions in Einstein–Weyl gravity, to showing that asymptotic safety can cure tidal divergences of extremal black holes, and to a new class of regular black holes with a hidden wormhole, derived from an action principle in four dimensions.

Testing asymptotic safety with chiral Lagrangians

Chiral Lagrangians are non-renormalizable EFTs whose UV completion (QCD) is known, which makes them an ideal laboratory. Going to next-to-leading order in the derivative expansion, I showed that the putative UV fixed point does not persist: asymptotic safety is not generic.

Hořava gravity

I reformulated the quantization of non-projectable Hořava gravity in a Lagrangian path-integral language and computed the one-loop running of its couplings in 2+1 dimensions. Next steps concern its renormalizability and whether the RG flow can dynamically restore Lorentz invariance at low energies. I am also studying interferometric signatures of Lorentz violation in khronometric gravity.

Black hole physics without quantum gravity

Black holes are the classic motivation for quantum gravity, yet it is unclear which aspects of their thermodynamics and evaporation really depend on the microscopic theory.

Robustness of Hawking radiation

I showed how universal horizons in Lorentz-violating gravity radiate in a purely geometric, species-independent way, how the relativistic Unruh vacuum is recovered at low energies, and how dispersion in analogue systems shows up as deviations from thermality.

Gravitational quantum friction and information loss

Treating collapsing matter quantum mechanically, already within Hawking’s semiclassical setup, opens a new interaction channel: the collapsing degrees of freedom are annihilated by Hawking partners just behind the horizon. This gravitational quantum friction can locally remove the trapping region and imprint information onto the outgoing radiation.

Non-circular black holes

For black holes whose horizons are not Killing horizons, I showed that Hawking-like emission persists but becomes anisotropic, and derived generalized laws of black hole mechanics with a new dissipative term.
